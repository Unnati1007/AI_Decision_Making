# Legal & Statutory Decision Framework: Employment Laws & Benefits (India)

## 📌 Executive Summary
Employment laws and statutory benefit regulations in India govern notice periods, provident fund contributions, gratuity entitlements, and non-compete enforceability.

## 💰 Statutory Benefits Breakdown

### 1. Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972
- **Eligibility**: Minimum 5 years of continuous service in an organization with 10+ employees (4 years 240 days counts as 5 years).
- **Calculation Formula**:
  $$\text{Gratuity} = \frac{\text{15} \times \text{Last Drawn Basic Salary} \times \text{Tenure (Years)}}{\text{26}}$$
- **Tax Exemption Limit**: Exempt up to ₹20 Lakhs under Section 10(10) of IT Act.

### 2. Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)
- **Contribution Split**: 12% of Basic + DA contributed by employee; matched 12% by employer.
  - Employer split: **3.67%** goes to EPF Account; **8.33%** (capped at ₹1,250/month) goes to Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS).
- **Taxability**: Employer contribution > ₹7.5 Lakhs/year across EPF/NPS is taxable. EPF interest is tax-free up to ₹2.5 Lakhs employee contribution/year.

## ⚖️ Notice Period & Non-Compete Clauses in India

### 1. Notice Period & Buyout Norms
- Standard notice periods range from 30 days to 90 days.
- **Buyout Provision**: If an employer or employee desires early release, the party compensating the basic salary for remaining days initiates a buyout. Buyout is subject to mutual employer consent.
- **Form 16 & Relieving Letter**: Employers are legally bound to issue Service Certificate and Form 16 upon settling full and final (F&F) accounts.

### 2. Enforceability of Non-Compete Clauses
- Under **Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872**, any agreement restraining anyone from exercising a lawful profession, trade, or business is **void and unenforceable**.
- Post-employment non-compete clauses prohibiting working for a competitor cannot be legally enforced in Indian courts (landmark case: *Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning*).
- **Non-Disclosure (NDA)** and **Non-Solicitation** clauses, however, remain legally enforceable to protect proprietary trade secrets and client lists.
